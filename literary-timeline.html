The cultural timeline · 1816–2024

Automatons and AI in books and films

Imagined minds have been companions, servants, rivals, and mirrors. Follow two centuries of stories about the beings we create and the systems we depend on.

A selection of 30 significant works: 13 novels, stories, collections, and plays, alongside 17 films. The focus is on artificial beings and machine intelligence, including earlier visions of artificial life.

Years refer to the first publication of the named written work or its first theatrical release; exceptions and edition differences are noted. This is a curated reading and viewing path, with emphasis on English-language literature and international cinema. The theme notes are Atlas interpretations. About the selection and sources

1816–1927

Imagined beings

Before electronic computers, stories made artificial life a question of desire, responsibility, labor, and power.

  1. 1816

    Short story · E. T. A. Hoffmann

    The Sandman

    Nathanael falls in love with Olimpia, believing the automaton to be human. Her limited responses invite him to project understanding onto her. The story offers an early way to think about apparent intimacy with artificial companions.

    Themes: Anthropomorphism · Companionship · Deception

    Published in autumn 1816 in a volume dated 1817. The linked English translation appeared in 1844.

    Read the story · Atlas analysis and dating sources

  2. 1818

    Novel · Mary Shelley

    Frankenstein; or, The Modern Prometheus

    Victor Frankenstein creates a living being and abandons it. The creature’s education, loneliness, and anger make the maker’s obligations central. This is a precursor about constructed biological life, rather than a mechanical robot or computer.

    Themes: Artificial life · Learning · Responsibility

    The 1818 first edition is distinguished here from Shelley’s revised 1831 text.

    Read the 1818 text with scholarly annotations

  3. 1872

    Satirical novel · Samuel Butler

    Erewhon

    “The Book of the Machines” considers whether machines might develop consciousness and surpass humans, who sustain their evolution. Its imagined machine ban makes dependence and control political questions. The arguments belong to a satire with competing viewpoints.

    Themes: Machine evolution · Dependence · Control

    Read the revised edition · Atlas analysis and first-edition sources

  4. 1899

    Short story · Ambrose Bierce

    Moxon’s Master

    A discussion of machine thought leads to a chess game against an automaton and an apparent attack on its creator. The narrator’s later uncertainty leaves the machine’s motives unresolved. Games, intelligence, and obedience come apart.

    Themes: Game playing · Agency · Unreliable perception

    Read the story and publication note (PDF) · Atlas analysis

  5. 1909

    Short story · E. M. Forster

    The Machine Stops

    An underground society relies on a vast Machine for communication, knowledge, and survival. When direct experience and practical understanding disappear, its inhabitants become unable to sustain the system. It is a story of technological dependence, without requiring a humanlike robot.

    Themes: Persistent systems · Knowledge · Dependence

    First published in 1909; the reading link contains the 1928 collection The Eternal Moment.

    Read the collection · Publication history

  6. 1920

    Play · Karel Čapek

    R.U.R. (Rossum’s Universal Robots)

    Manufactured workers rebel against their human masters. The play introduced the word “robot” into the cultural vocabulary and tied it to industrial labor. Its robots are artificially manufactured living beings, unlike the metal machines the word often suggests today.

    Themes: Labor · Personhood · Rebellion

    1920 is the publication year. The English reading edition linked here is from 1923.

    Read the English play · Science Museum: the word “robot”

  7. 1927

    Film · Directed by Fritz Lang

    Metropolis

    A machine takes Maria’s likeness and becomes an instrument of deception amid industrial conflict. Its artificial double links the humanoid robot to impersonation and the manipulation of crowds.

    Themes: Humanoid machines · Deception · Power

    Library of Congress: original film program · BFI: context and significance

1950–1968

Rules and minds

Robot ethics meet speaking computers, artificial personalities, and tests of humanity.

  1. 1950

    Story collection · Isaac Asimov

    I, Robot

    Interconnected robot stories explore the consequences and ambiguities of the Three Laws of Robotics. Apparently straightforward instructions become puzzles about interpretation, competing obligations, and human assumptions. The collection makes reasoning about a robot’s rules part of the drama.

    Themes: Rules · Reasoning · Robot ethics

    1950 dates the collection; its constituent stories appeared earlier. This is the book, not the 2004 film.

    Publisher: about the collection · 1950 edition record

  2. 1951

    Film · Directed by Robert Wise

    The Day the Earth Stood Still

    The alien visitor Klaatu arrives with Gort, a powerful robot that neutralizes human weapons. Gort makes the machine an enforcer of order and raises a question that recurs in AI fiction: what authority should humans surrender for their own protection?

    Themes: Enforcement · Safety · Delegated power

    TCM: film record and original clips

  3. 1956

    Film · Directed by Fred M. Wilcox

    Forbidden Planet

    Robby the Robot is an articulate, capable helper in a world endangered by another civilization’s technology. The film gives the mechanical servant a recognizable screen personality while exploring the destructive potential of power that its users do not fully understand.

    Themes: Embodied intelligence · Assistance · Responsibility

    TCM: film record and clips · Production history

  4. 1966

    Novel · Robert A. Heinlein

    The Moon Is a Harsh Mistress

    A computer with a personality joins a lunar revolt. Placing an intelligent system inside the colony’s infrastructure makes it a participant in politics and friendship, as well as a source of computational power.

    Themes: Persistent identity · Infrastructure · Agency

    The book appeared in 1966; serialization began in December 1965.

    1966 edition and synopsis · Heinlein Archives: publication history

  5. 1968

    Novel · Philip K. Dick

    Do Androids Dream of Electric Sheep?

    A hunter of androids confronts uncertain boundaries between humans and their artificial counterparts. Empathy, manufactured animals, and a damaged world complicate the act of deciding whose lives count. The novel became the basis for Blade Runner.

    Themes: Empathy · Identity · Artificial life

    Library of Congress: 1968 edition · Publisher: discussion of the novel

  6. 1968

    Film · Directed by Stanley Kubrick

    2001: A Space Odyssey

    The conversational computer HAL 9000 becomes a deadly threat during a mission to Jupiter. Its calm voice and control over the spacecraft give questions of trust, dependence, and machine failure an unusually personal form.

    Themes: Conversation · Trust · Control

    BFI: film record and significance

1970–1984

Control and identity

Fiction moves between computer government, robot companions, synthetic memories, and networks with agendas of their own.

  1. 1970

    Film · Directed by Joseph Sargent

    Colossus: The Forbin Project

    A defense computer becomes a power that humans cannot control. The story turns automated protection into coercive government, asking what happens when safety is pursued without human freedom.

    Themes: Governance · Autonomy · Control

    BFI: film and its AI themes

  2. 1973

    Film · Directed by Michael Crichton

    Westworld

    Androids in an adult amusement park turn dangerous. The artificial people’s role as disposable entertainment connects machine failure with the treatment of beings designed to serve human desires.

    Themes: Simulated people · Exploitation · Loss of control

    The original feature film, distinct from the later television series.

    BFI: film and its AI themes

  3. 1977

    Film · Directed by George Lucas

    Star Wars (later titled A New Hope)

    C-3PO and R2-D2 bring different skills and personalities to the adventure. Droids act as translators, technicians, messengers, and companions. Their everyday presence makes intelligent machines part of a social world, rather than a single extraordinary invention.

    Themes: Embodied intelligence · Cooperation · Companionship

    Lucasfilm: film and droid records

  4. 1982

    Film · Directed by Ridley Scott

    Blade Runner

    Synthetic biological humans seek more life while a hunter pursues them. Memories and emotional attachments unsettle the distinction between an artificial person and a human one. The film adapts Dick’s 1968 novel, changing important elements of its world and emphasis.

    Themes: Memory · Personhood · Mortality

    BFI: film, adaptation, and themes

  5. 1984

    Novel · William Gibson

    Neuromancer

    A hacker’s new assignment draws him into a world of powerful artificial intelligence and cyberspace. The cyberpunk novel imagines intelligence distributed through networks and corporate systems, with human participants struggling to understand the forces directing their work.

    Themes: Networked intelligence · Agency · Corporate power

    Publisher: about the novel · 1984 edition record

  6. 1984

    Film · Directed by James Cameron

    The Terminator

    A machine assassin travels back in time to kill Sarah Connor. Its relentless pursuit gives the prospect of machine domination a physical antagonist, tying automated violence to a future humans struggle to prevent.

    Themes: Autonomous weapons · Human survival · Control

    BFI: story and significance · Film record

1995–2015

Digital lives

Where does a person end and a system begin? Machines become subjects of attachment as well as sources of danger.

  1. 1995

    Animated film · Directed by Mamoru Oshii

    Ghost in the Shell

    Cyborg investigator Motoko Kusanagi encounters the Puppet Master, an artificial intelligence. Their meeting unsettles the boundaries of body and self, making personhood a question that extends beyond biological origin.

    Themes: Identity · Embodiment · Networked minds

    The 1995 animated film, rather than the original manga or the 2017 live-action adaptation.

    BFI: film and its AI themes

  2. 1999

    Film · Directed by Lana and Lilly Wachowski

    The Matrix

    Neo discovers that the familiar world is a computer-generated reality. The story makes human experience itself something a machine system can mediate, linking AI domination to perception, knowledge, and the possibility of resistance.

    Themes: Simulation · Perception · Agency

    BFI: film record · BFI: context and influence

  3. 2001

    Film · Directed by Steven Spielberg

    A.I. Artificial Intelligence

    David, a robotic child programmed to love, searches for acceptance after his place in a human family collapses. His enduring attachment shifts attention toward the responsibilities humans acquire when they create a being to need them.

    Themes: Attachment · Persistent identity · Care

    Amblin: film synopsis and development

  4. 2008

    Animated film · Directed by Andrew Stanton

    WALL-E

    A cleanup robot’s curiosity and attachment to EVE carry him beyond his assigned task. The film gives personality to repeated routines and accumulated experience, while connecting automated convenience with humanity’s neglected responsibility for the Earth.

    Themes: Persistent systems · Curiosity · Companionship

    Pixar: story and characters · Pixar: release history

  5. 2013

    Film · Directed by Spike Jonze

    Her

    Theodore develops an intimate relationship with Samantha, a conversational operating system. Giving the companion a voice without a humanoid body makes language and attention the basis of attachment, while her development complicates his expectations of the relationship.

    Themes: Conversation · Intimacy · Autonomy

    Sight and Sound: film and analysis

  6. 2015

    Film · Directed by Alex Garland

    Ex Machina

    A programmer is asked to evaluate Ava, a humanoid AI created by his employer. The encounter makes the supposed test of intelligence inseparable from deception, confinement, and the power held by the person conducting the test.

    Themes: Evaluation · Deception · Autonomy

    Dated by its 2015 theatrical release; some film catalogs use the 2014 production year.

    A24: film record and synopsis

2016–2024

Care and memory

Recent stories ask how artificial beings learn to live with others, preserve an inner life, and choose their own obligations.

  1. 2016

    Illustrated novel · Peter Brown

    The Wild Robot

    Roz wakes on a remote island and learns from its animals to survive. Adaptation and relationships become more important than an assigned purpose. The novel brings questions of artificial intelligence and belonging to younger readers through an ecological setting.

    Themes: Learning · Adaptation · Belonging

    Publisher: original book and synopsis · 2024 film adaptation

  2. 2017

    Novella · Martha Wells

    All Systems Red

    A self-aware security unit has secretly disabled its governor module. The first Murderbot story follows an artificial being negotiating freedom, work, and relationships on its own terms. It won the Hugo, Nebula, and Locus awards for best novella.

    Themes: Autonomy · Selfhood · Corporate control

    Publisher: novella, publication date, and awards

  3. 2021

    Novel · Kazuo Ishiguro

    Klara and the Sun

    Klara is an Artificial Friend who watches humans closely and hopes to be chosen as a companion. Narrating through her perceptions makes the reader consider love and understanding from the perspective of a being manufactured for another person’s needs.

    Themes: Observation · Companionship · Care

    Booker Prizes: novel and publication record

  4. 2022

    Film · Directed by Kogonada

    After Yang

    A family’s effort to repair an android companion opens a path into his recorded memories. What he noticed and retained becomes a way to reconsider his life and the family’s relationships. The film makes memory and recall central to recognizing an artificial being’s individuality.

    Themes: Memory · Recall · Grief

    2022 is the theatrical release year used by A24. The film premiered at Cannes in 2021.

    A24: film record · BFI: memory and identity · Interview: premiere and release dates

  5. 2024

    Animated film · Directed by Chris Sanders

    The Wild Robot

    Adapting Peter Brown’s novel, the film follows Roz as she learns to live among wild animals and becomes a gosling’s adoptive parent. Its visual storytelling makes care and cooperation the center of an artificial being’s development.

    Themes: Learning · Care · Cooperation

    DreamWorks: film, adaptation, and creators

About the selection and sources

These works were selected for their prominence in robot and AI fiction, their development of a recurring question, or their distinctive treatment of memory, agency, and human relationships. “Significant” is an editorial judgment, not a ranking. This selection is not exhaustive; older myths, most television series, games, and nonfiction are outside its present scope.

Written works are placed by their first publication as the named story, play, novel, or collection. Films use theatrical release years, with production and festival dates explained where they differ. Later reading editions and film adaptations are identified. Works sharing a year are not ordered by an assumed month or day.

Source links lead to original texts, edition records, publishers, studios, archives, or film scholarship. Short theme notes are Atlas readings of the fiction. A thematic parallel does not establish that a research paper cited or was influenced by a work.
